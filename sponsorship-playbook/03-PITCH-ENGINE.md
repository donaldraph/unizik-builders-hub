## 3. Objection Handling Matrix

### Objection 1: "We don't have budget for university events."

| | |
|---|---|
| **Underlying Concern** | The company sees university events as low-priority spend with unclear returns, or sponsorship budgets are genuinely exhausted. |
| **Rebuttal** | I completely understand — that's why we've structured this so AWS carries the primary cost ($5,000+ in grants already confirmed). We're not asking you to fund an event from scratch. We're offering you a seat at a table that's already set. For as little as N300,000 (~$200), you get logo visibility, a demo booth, and direct access to 300-500 students who are your future users and hires. |
| **Pivot Question** | "If budget is tight for cash, would in-kind support — like branded merch, data bundles, or voucher codes — be more feasible? We'd still give you full sponsor branding." |

---

### Objection 2: "Why Awka/UNIZIK instead of Lagos/Abuja?"

| | |
|---|---|
| **Underlying Concern** | The prospect assumes Lagos and Abuja are the only markets worth investing in. They underestimate the South-East's tech growth. |
| **Rebuttal** | That's actually exactly why this is valuable. Lagos is saturated — every fintech and every tech community is fighting for the same eyeballs. Awka is underserved and growing fast. UNIZIK has 36,000 students, Anambra State has one of Nigeria's highest entrepreneurship rates, and we're the *only* AWS Student Builder Group in the region. You'd be the first brand to establish a developer presence here — zero competition for share of mind. |
| **Pivot Question** | "Which metric would make an Awka activation worthwhile for you — sign-ups, app downloads, or API integrations? We can design the activation around that." |

---

### Objection 3: "What immediate ROI will we see from student developers?"

| | |
|---|---|
| **Underlying Concern** | They want measurable outcomes, not vague "brand awareness." Reasonable ask. |
| **Rebuttal** | Totally fair question. Here's what we can deliver and track: direct app downloads or account sign-ups at on-site activation booths (with QR codes and unique referral links), social media impressions across our event channels, and a post-event impact report with exact numbers within 72 hours. Beyond the event itself, students who adopt your product during university become long-term users — 80% of students stick with the first bank, payment tool, or savings app they onboard with. |
| **Pivot Question** | "If we set up tracked referral links so you can see exactly how many sign-ups come from our event, would that address the ROI question?" |

---

### Objection 4: "We already sponsor GDG/other communities."

| | |
|---|---|
| **Underlying Concern** | They think all student tech communities are the same and they've already checked the box. |
| **Rebuttal** | That's great — it shows you understand the value of student developer communities. Here's what makes us different: AWS Student Builder Groups are backed directly by Amazon Web Services with grants, premium Skill Builder access, and global infrastructure. Our members are specifically learning cloud architecture, AI/ML, and serverless — they're the students building production-ready applications, not just attending talks. GDG and AWS SBG attract overlapping but distinct audiences; sponsoring both gives you broader coverage, not redundancy. |
| **Pivot Question** | "Would it help if I shared our member profile breakdown — the tech stacks they use, the projects they're building, and how it complements your GDG investment?" |

---

---
